# 03.4.2.5. Preserve ownership inside repeatedly allocated shared payloads

In `fresh_buffers_charge_each_outstanding_visit`, wrap the fresh
`Arc<Vec<u8>>` in another `Arc` before sending it to its worker. With a
generous budget the executable `handles.push(job)` at revision `8e4dde1f8`
rejects an “indexed Arc payload with incoming storage or nested reference
lifetimes”. The structure is acyclic and every handle has a destructor.

- [x] Carry the inner ownership description with the outer allocation's full
  visit identity. Cover a locally owned buffer, incoming storage, two inner
  aliases and an embedded join handle. Keep the input charged to its caller.
- [x] Compare distinct inner allocations with two outer allocations sharing
  one inner root. Hold an independent alias through the outer drop. Verify
  final release, reject a premature one-byte reduction and retain the lost-job
  rejection when the payload still owns a worker handle.
- [x] Run the applicable checks and record the revision in the
  [ownership audit](03.4.2-audit-loops-and-shared-ownership.md). Reuse the
  existing fixed-payload tests in `worker_shared_ownership/payloads.rs` as
  controls for the repeated-site cases.

## Evidence

Feature commit `2cf864351` verifies the original nested-buffer program. For two
19-byte buffers the coordinator proves 214 live bytes with both jobs outstanding,
97 with one joined result left, and 6 after its drop. The 213- and 96-byte
claims fail. With capacities that vary by visit, the first nested result retains
its own 7-byte buffer after the later result drops.

The regressions are in `worker_shared_ownership/occurrences/payloads.rs`.
Two private paths to one buffer charge it once. With two fresh wrappers around
one root created before the loop, the coordinator proves 177 bytes with both
jobs outstanding and 71 while an independent inner alias survives both outer
drops. Dropping the publisher's alias first also verifies. A closed consumer
takes distinct incoming buffers on successive visits. Its caller needs 586
bytes for a 512-byte call and 74 bytes of original storage, rejects 585 and
reuses the released storage on return.

A fresh `Arc<JoinHandle<_>>` preserves the embedded job's actual publication.
Two visits return 8- and 7-byte buffers in pop order. The 102- and 75-byte
checkpoints verify, and lowering either by one fails. Extracting with a live
clone or discarding an unjoined job also fails. The global concurrent-peak
obligation remains open.
