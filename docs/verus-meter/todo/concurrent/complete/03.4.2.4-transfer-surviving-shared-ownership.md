# 03.4.2.4. Transfer surviving shared ownership

Extend [03.4.2.3](03.4.2.3-join-shared-collections-in-a-worker.md) to collectors
that return outstanding handles or aliases and to a fresh allocation whose
carrying result moves to another actor while a peer still holds its own alias.

- [x] Drain a captured mixed collection to a symbolic remainder. Return the
  remainder or a selected alias and check what remains live at each collection.
  Preserve the original checked publication, source inputs and result identity.
- [x] In `an_equal_sized_buffer_from_another_visit_cannot_take_the_charge`,
  replace `drop(later)` by spawning and joining a worker that drops `later`.
  Revision `8e4dde1f8` reports “forwarding a returned Arc with indexed peer
  reservations to another actor” at spawn. Preserve the 222-, 214- and 146-byte
  checkpoints. Exercise returning the alias as well as consuming it.
- [x] Cover zero, partial and final collection. Reject a one-byte shortfall,
  a lost handle and release of another visit's equal-sized backing. Keep the
  global proof gate. Record tests and revision in the
  [ownership audit](03.4.2-audit-loops-and-shared-ownership.md).

## Evidence

In `957857b33`, a collector's join replaces the references in its captured
handle slots with the references carried by its actual result.
`collector_returns_the_remaining_handle_slots` drains to a symbolic length.
For one pair of workers it proves 46, 94 and 154 live bytes with zero, one and
two slots left. It rejects 93 bytes at one slot and rejects discarding the
remaining handle. `collected_arc_keeps_the_selected_workers_result_identity`
follows the first publication through a symbolic drain. Swapping two concrete
workers' captures changes which backing the same collector returns.

In `c7bcde2bb`, `a_forwarded_carrier_keeps_its_original_visit` hands the
carrying alias to a fixed worker. Consuming and returning it both preserve
the 222-, 214- and 146-byte checkpoints. With the returned `Arc` still in
scope, the publisher has 230 live bytes. The verifier rejects 229 there and 213 after the other visit's peer
ends. A receiving worker stored in another handle vector is rejected at the
push. All positive programs reach the existing global concurrent-peak gate.
